7 CRM automations that pay for themselves in the first month

Updated October 26, 2026 · Kai Team · 6 min read

A CRM automation is an event → action rule that runs without the salesperson: a deal reaches a stage and a task appears, a client goes quiet and a reminder fires. A good one closes a specific hole that money leaks through: forgotten enquiries, stuck deals, a report with no loss reasons. Here are seven rules that usually pay for themselves in the first month for a small sales team, where each runs, and the phrase that switches it on in Kai.

TL;DR

  • Start with two rules: a task on stage change and a no-reply reminder.
  • A rule should fit in one sentence; if it needs a diagram, it is a business process.
  • Some rules live inside the CRM (Digital Pipeline, Bitrix24 robots), some on Kai's side.
  • In Kai, event rules are part of Pro (€59/month); the 3-day trial runs on Start features.

Seven rules to start with

1. A task when a deal reaches a stage

Problem. The rep moves a deal to "Quote sent" and nobody calls back.
Rule. Deal reaches stage X → task for the owner "call back", due in 2 days; one per working stage.
Where it runs. amoCRM and Kommo: Digital Pipeline on plans that include it; Bitrix24: robots. Kai: a rule on its side that listens to CRM events and creates the task through the API.

When a deal moves to "Quote sent", set a task "call back" due in 2 days.

2. A no-reply reminder

Problem. The client writes, the rep is in a meeting, and forty minutes later the client messages a competitor.
Rule. No reply for N minutes → a task on the deal or a notification.
Where it runs. Inside the CRM: chatbots (Salesbot in Kommo) with connected messengers. Kai: "no reply for 30 minutes → task on the deal", when the conversation runs through the CRM.

If a client waits 30 minutes for a reply, set a task on the deal and notify me.

3. A stuck-deal alert

Problem. Stage "Thinking", 37 days without movement, still counted as alive.
Rule. No movement for N days → task "update the status" or a notification.
Where it runs. Partly inside the CRM ("no task" filters, Digital Pipeline timers), mostly by eye. Kai: a rule on its side plus the daily funnel health check that shows where deals pile up.

Deal with no movement for 14 days → task for the rep "update the status" and notify me.

4. A new-lead notification in the team chat

Problem. The enquiry arrives at 7 pm and is seen at 10 am; the first to answer wins.
Rule. New deal → message in Telegram with name, phone and source.
Where it runs. The CRM has in-app notifications; the team's messenger needs a connector or an assistant. Kai sends it straight into the chat, no separate bot or tokens; Pro adds Telegram, Slack, Discord and Google Sheets.

New deal in the "Website" pipeline → notify me in Telegram: name, phone, source.

5. Source and loss reason are always filled in

Problem. A month in, most deals have an empty "Source" and lost deals have no reason.
Rule. No leaving the first stage without a source, no closing as lost without a loss reason.
Where it runs. Inside the CRM: required fields per stage (availability depends on your CRM plan); forms and messengers often set the source themselves. Kai creates the lists, makes the fields required, and its daily health check finds deals without a reason and fixes the lists in one tap.

Make "Source" required to leave "New enquiry" and "Loss reason" required at close. Reasons: too expensive, chose a competitor, no response.

6. A weekly funnel digest

Problem. The report is assembled by hand on Friday evening and is stale by Monday.
Rule. Once a week → deals per stage, where they get stuck, loss reasons, deals without tasks.
Where it runs. CRM dashboards you open yourself. Kai checks funnel health daily, grades it A–D and sends a weekly summary to the chat; if all is well it stays quiet, and the check does not spend requests.

Turn on the weekly funnel health summary in this chat.

7. A webhook to an external tool

Problem. The deal is won and someone copies the order by hand into the shipping sheet.
Rule. Deal moves to "Paid" → deal data goes by webhook to your service or into Google Sheets.
Where it runs. The CRM can send a webhook, but something must receive it. In Kai, a Google Sheets row is a Pro integration; a webhook to your own service and multi-step chains are a custom build on Max, €30 flat per scenario.

When a deal moves to "Paid", send a webhook to https://example.com/hook with the deal name, amount and phone.

Where the rules live: in the CRM or in Kai

Digital Pipeline and robots live inside the CRM: configured there, running as long as the CRM is paid for, but needing a plan with automations and time spent clicking. Kai's rules live on Kai's side: it listens to CRM events via a webhook and acts through the API, so they switch on and change with one phrase, but do not appear in Digital Pipeline and run while your subscription is active. Whatever they have created in the CRM stays forever.

For Bitrix24 Kai is in beta: no robots inside Bitrix24, only notifications about the main events via the outgoing webhook. If in-Bitrix24 automations are your main need, set up robots by hand or with an integrator.

FAQ

How do Kai's rules differ from Digital Pipeline in amoCRM and Kommo?

Digital Pipeline lives inside the CRM and is configured there on the plans that include it. Kai's rules live on Kai's side and act through the API, so they switch on with one phrase and run while the subscription is active. Everything created in the CRM stays.

How many automations does a small sales team need?

Two or three to start: a task on stage change, a no-reply reminder and a new-lead notification. Add the rest when the weekly digest shows a hole; more than ten rules on one pipeline means the process needs rethinking.

Do I need the Pro plan to switch on automations in Kai?

Yes. Event rules, roles and the standard integrations (Telegram, Slack, Discord, Google Sheets) are Pro at €59/month; the 3-day trial runs on Start features. A webhook to your own service and multi-step chains are Max at €119/month.

What happens to the rules if I disconnect Kai?

They run on Kai's side, so they stop after disconnection; everything already created in the CRM stays. For rules inside the CRM, use Digital Pipeline or Bitrix24 robots, by hand or with an integrator.

What next

Pick two rules (almost always the first two) and switch them on today, in Digital Pipeline or with one phrase in the Kai chat. Automate what reminds a person before what does the work for them; auto-replies and mass mailings can wait. A week later, open the digest and count the tasks.
